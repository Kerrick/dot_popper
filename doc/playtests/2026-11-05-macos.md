# Playtest: macOS, November 5, 2026

The maintainer played Dot Popper v0.8.2-rc.1 on his wife's MacBook Air (15-inch, M4, 2025, macOS 27.0.1), on its built-in 60 Hz display, with no controller. It was the macOS kit's app, fused by `lota fuse` from Lota 0.1.3's release gem for arm64-darwin, started from Terminal with `DOT_POPPER_PERF=1`. He filled in the kit's `macos-results.txt` as he went.

## What worked

- Sound plays, the mute survives quitting and starting again, and M mutes during a game too ("which is nice").
- Command+Enter switches fullscreen on and off with every dot kept on screen, and so does resizing the window mid-game.
- Pops pan left and right, as far as a laptop's speakers let him tell.
- The bright dot's edge is easy to see while it moves, and the text looks sharp.
- The Dot size choice changes the title's dots, and the next game's match.
- Copy my result pastes cleanly into TextEdit:

  ```text
  Dot Popper: 31 points, a new best!
  New record: 18 dots in a row without a mistake
  🟡 35 pops · ⚫ 2 mistakes · ⏱ 41 seconds
  Played with dots sized to the screen.
  ```

- "Your records" opens the records, and a click goes back. Dragging a pile dot throws it, and a plain click on Game Over goes to the title.
- Overall: 4.8 of 5.

## His notes, in his words

- Clicking near an edge: "If I get _too_ close to the edge, the target is much smaller (because circles) and I can't do it. But if I'm at least like a third of a dot-width away, I can hit the dot."
- Resizing: "It stopped running the game while I was dragging the resize handle, but after I stopped it rearranged properly. In-game "time" didn't advance while it was frozen that way."
- Panning: "I think so; the laptop speakers aren't that far apart, and leaning my head closer made it hard to pay attention to the dots and click them."
- The bright dot's edge: "It's not as crisp and clean looking compared to my RTX 4090 Windows PC with 120Hz OLED. I don't know if that's because of the awesome hardware on my PC setup, or if the Mac has a low FPS or what."
- Dot size: "At the default window size, the Dot Size option didn't appear. I had to make it bigger or smaller to get the two options to show up."
- Your records: "I didn't get any kind of hover indicator on Your Records. (Or as far as I can recall, any other clickable elements.)"
- Sharp text: "I think so? It's hard to tell with such a bubbly/rounded font."

## His games

`games.csv` logged 19 games in 13 minutes, all on the "score 40 in a long game" goal. The best was 40 points (44 pops, 2 mistakes, in a 50-second game), and the goal was met twice, with 31 and with 40. Seven were short and quiet, with no mistake and seven pops or fewer, most likely while he tried a check. Six ended at 0 within six and a half seconds, after three to five mistakes, with the floor taking what the score couldn't pay. In the five games he played out (19 to 40 points), he waited about 1.1 seconds on average for the bright dot to come clear.

## Performance

`DOT_POPPER_PERF=1` reported a steady 60 frames a second, with the longest frame under 46 ms in each five seconds, except right after a fullscreen switch. There, two frames of 624 ms and 583 ms came on Game Over, 0.0 s after the switch, with no garbage collection in either, and that five seconds averaged 47 frames a second. The slow-frame report added after the Windows playtest confirms what that playtest only suspected about its 535 ms frame: the pause is the window changing mode, not the game.

## What he found, and what it led to

| Finding | Change |
| --- | --- |
| No hover feedback on "Your records" or any other button. | Every button (the title's "Your records", Dot size segments, and speaker; Game Over's "Copy my result"; the Stats screen's "Clear my records…" and Undo) now brightens its backing and outline while the mouse is over it, and the pointer becomes the system's pointing hand. Taps and hover share one list of hit areas, and only the mouse hovers (doc/decisions.md, "A mouse lights up the button under it"). |
| The game stops while a window edge is dragged, and a fullscreen switch pauses it for about 600 ms. | None in the game: macOS runs its own loop while a window is resized, and the slow frames come from the switch. Reported to Lota as an engine matter. The rules lose nothing, since the clock stands still meanwhile. |
| The bright dot's edge looks less crisp than on a 120 Hz OLED. | None. The Mac drew a steady 60 frames a second; the difference is the display's refresh rate and pixel response, not the game. |
| The Dot size choice didn't show at the default window size. | None yet. That is by design: the choice is offered only where the two sizes look different, and at the default window they don't. The maintainer will decide whether to show it anyway. |
| A dot touching the window's edge is hard to hit from that side. | None; expected. He explained that clicks don't fail: a dot pressed against the edge leaves only a thin sliver beside it to aim at, so he sometimes missed it, and a third of a dot-width away it was easy. |
| The kit's header said "version 0.8.1" for v0.8.2-rc.1, since the version came from `fledge.yml`. | Each tagged kit now takes the tag's version (a release candidate's base version), and the Kits workflow checks the header names it. |
