# Playtest: Windows, November 5, 2026

The first time a person played Dot Popper. It was v0.8.1, played by the maintainer on his own PC with a kit that recorded the machine, ran the game, and asked a list of questions as he played. This page keeps what he found and what each finding led to, so later playtests have something to compare against.

## The setup

- Windows 11 on x64, with an RTX 4090 drawing through Vulkan.
- A 3840 by 2160 TV at 200% display scaling, so the game saw a 1920 by 1080 screen in logical pixels.
- An Xbox Elite Series 2 controller, a mouse, and a keyboard.
- Sound from a short soundbar.

## What worked

- The game drew at a steady 120 frames a second, matching the display. Its automatic run and its run without shaders both ended cleanly, and the installer installed, started, reinstalled, and uninstalled without trouble.
- Clicking dots near every edge popped them. F11 and Alt+Enter switched fullscreen, and the dots stayed on screen.
- Copy my result, Your records, sharp text at 200%, and dragging the Game Over pile with the mouse all worked.
- The controller's cursor, A to pop, and the Elite's back paddle mapped to A all worked.
- He rated the game 5 out of 5 overall.

## Sound

He heard the game through the soundbar. Asked afterwards:

- Pops arrived with the click, or close enough that he couldn't tell the difference.
- The pops were satisfying, and the low sound of a mistake was easy to tell apart from a pop.
- The tick in the last three seconds added urgency without annoying him. He particularly liked that he could make it stop by playing better: each pop adds a second, which lifts the clock back out of the last three. The tick reports the state of the game and rewards skill, so it should stay.
- Nothing crackled, dropped out, or distorted.
- Stereo panning couldn't be judged, because a short soundbar has little stereo separation. The mute check was skipped.

## What he found, and what it led to

| Finding | Cause | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At fullscreen, "You met today's goal!" was drawn over "Time's up!" on Game Over. | Each line sat at a fixed share of the screen's height, and the goal line didn't move down when the stacking lesson pushed the heading down. | The title and Game Over now stack their lines in order and shrink or leave out the least important ones when room runs short. |
| On a small window, the title's lines overlapped one another and the Dot size choice. | The same fixed shares of the height, on a short window. | The same stacking. |
| "Tap" is the word for a touchscreen; a mouse clicks, and a controller needs another verb. | The words were written for touch alone. | The words follow the last input used: "Click" with a mouse, "Tap" on touch, and the button's name, such as "Press A", on a controller. "Wrong tap" became "mistake" everywhere. |
| The controller's cursor felt right on smaller windows but far too slow at fullscreen. | Its speed was fixed in dots a second, so it took about 2.4 seconds to cross a fullscreen 4K TV. | At full tilt it now crosses any window's diagonal in about a second, and it builds up to that speed. |
| Dragging the pile worked with the mouse but not with the controller. | The drag was written for pointers alone. | Holding A on a pile dot now grabs it, and letting go throws it. |
| The game froze while he resized the window mid-round, with nothing moving while it was frozen. | Windows holds an app's loop while a window's edge is dragged. The game's cap of a tenth of a second a frame kept the clock from losing time. | None in the game. Whether Lota can keep drawing during a resize, as LÖVE does on some systems, is a question for Lota. |
| A few frames of 54 to 110 ms, and one of 535 ms, while he played by hand. | Unknown. They weren't the game's own screenshots, which were off for that run. | DOT_POPPER_PERF=1 now prints, for any frame of 50 ms or more, the screen it fell on, what happened just before, and how many garbage collections ran during it. |
