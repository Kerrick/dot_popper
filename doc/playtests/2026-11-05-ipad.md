# Playtest: iPad, November 5, 2026

A playtester played Dot Popper v0.8.2 on an iPad Pro (12.9-inch, 6th generation, iPadOS 26.7.1). It was the experimental build from Lota 0.1.3's iOS engine, installed from a Mac with Xcode's command-line tools and signed with a free Personal Team. She had played an earlier version that morning, and she knows the original Dot Popper.

## What she did and said

- She laughed and shouted "Oh no!" in a fun way while playing, and had a blast.
- Afterwards: "This is SUCH AN UPGRADE to O.G. Dot Popper!"

## What it tells us

- The build installs, launches, draws, and takes touch input on a real iPad. Before this, it had only run in the iPad Simulator.
- "Oh no!" said with a laugh is the reaction the stacking rule is meant to provoke: a mistake the player can see was her own, followed at once by wanting another go. The dashed outline that shows the bright dot a wrong tap was hiding, the plain stacking lesson, and the clock that a good player can keep out of the last three seconds all feed it. Changes to any of them should be checked against this.

## Her checklist

All of it is in `ipad-results.txt` in the shared results folder; in short:

- Fullscreen with no status bar in both orientations, and a tap pops exactly where the finger is.
- Dot size: right. "It _looks_ a little large, but with how fast they're moving I think it's the right size for _play_. I don't want it any smaller."
- Sound plays, mixes with another app's audio (a text-to-speech reader), and goes quiet in Silent Mode.
- Rotating mid-game keeps every dot on screen.
- Tilt rolls the pile, dragging throws a dot, a tap continues, the Dot size choice works, and Copy my result pastes cleanly.
- Her Xbox controller works: the cursor, A, Start, dragging with A, and the right stick, which she called "kinda weird, but actually kinda fun. Okay it's actually REALLY fun".
- Nothing crashed, froze, or looked wrong. Overall: 5 of 5.
- "It's a poopin' game for sure": a few minutes at a time, picked up and put down, every day. Keep a new game one tap away, and add nothing that makes a session longer.
- While playing she noticed that the popped dots look a little smaller than the bright one. She was sure they aren't, and they aren't: their light rim, blue body, and darker crater make them read as smaller, so the bright dot stands out. Keep that when changing the dots' look; their hit circles are, and must stay, the same.

## What she found, and what it led to

| Finding | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Once today's goal was met, the title said only "Today's goal is done.", so she tapped it to see what the goal had been and was dropped into a game. | The title now says "Done today:" and the goal itself. |
| "23 points short of your record of 28" read as awkward. | Game Over names the gap only within three points ("Just 2 points short of your record of 28"); otherwise it says "Your record is 28". |
| A shake at Game Over didn't seem to do anything beyond the tilt a hand makes while shaking. | A gentler jolt now counts as a shake, and it throws the dots several times their fastest speed. |
| Tilting is enjoyable with rotation locked, but with rotation on, tilting turns the screen, so she held the iPad flat and the dots rolled slowly. | None. She judged it sensible as it is. |
| A bright dot spawned almost entirely under a popped dot moving at nearly the same velocity, and her time ran out while it stayed hidden. | The maintainer approved a narrow spawn rule. A new dot that would offer no 44-point touch target within its first second gets a new velocity, or failing that a new place ([ADR-0002](../adr/0002-a-new-dot-offers-a-touch-target-within-a-second.md)). On an 11-inch iPad that is about 1 spawn in 200. |
| "There's gotta be a multiplayer mode where there's two different colors, somehow." | An idea for later; the hard part, as she said, is knowing whose mistake was whose. |
